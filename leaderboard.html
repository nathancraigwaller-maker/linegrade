The Verified Record

A "verified 58% record" tells you nothing. We grade handicappers on the one number they can't cherry-pick or take back: did they beat the price they posted — on picks we logged before the match was played?

Win-rate lies

You can hit 60% betting favorites and still lose money. A raw record is marketing, not proof.

The line is truth

The closing price is the market's final, sharpest estimate. Beating it consistently (CLV) is the only durable proof of skill.

We only count what's honest

Picks we logged before the result — never backfilled ones a leaderboard selected after the fact. Survivorship gets stripped out.

The Claimed record is every moneyline pick on record, including ones already settled when we first saw them — the flattering, survivorship-inflated number a service would advertise. The Verified column applies a strict gate: we count a pick only if we recorded it before the match was played. A pick simply sitting unsettled when we found it is not enough — boards settle late, so a months-old result can still read as pending. Those are counted as claimed, not verified. z = standard deviations above what the odds themselves implied; z ≥ 2 over 30–50 verified picks = a real, followable edge. Until then, the honest grade is Tracking.

What this does not measure yet. Picks are graded against the odds attached to them when posted, not against the closing line. True closing-line value — whether the market moved toward a pick after it was called — is the next thing being added, reconstructed from exchange price history. Until it ships, nothing here claims to be CLV.
